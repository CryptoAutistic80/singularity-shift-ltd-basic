Holiday Accommodation Websites in Cheltenham: Answer Questions Before Guests Book

Plan a Cheltenham holiday accommodation website with clear property details, availability, access information and a dependable booking or enquiry route.

By James Walford · Website designer & software developer, Cheltenham

4 min read · For: Owners and managers of holiday accommodation in and around Cheltenham.

In this guide

  1. Explain the space, not just its atmosphere
  2. Decide how availability will be maintained
  3. Make the total booking decision understandable
  4. Example: a family checking whether the layout works
  5. Be precise about location and arrival
  6. Prepare a property information checklist

Choosing somewhere to stay involves more than liking a photograph. A guest needs to work out whether the space suits their group, what is included, whether the dates are available and how arrival will work.

A holiday accommodation website in Cheltenham should make those decisions easier. The most useful brief starts with the questions guests ask before booking and the misunderstandings that create extra work after they arrive.

Explain the space, not just its atmosphere

Give each property or room enough information for someone to assess it. "Sleeps four" does not explain whether there are two bedrooms, a sofa bed in a shared area or beds that can be arranged differently.

Describe the layout, bed arrangements, bathrooms and shared spaces accurately. State which facilities are private and which are shared. If a facility is seasonal or must be requested, make that clear beside the description.

Photographs should support those facts. Include useful views of bedrooms, bathrooms, living areas and entrances rather than several nearly identical decorative details. Avoid presenting neighbouring spaces or views as part of the property unless the distinction is explicit.

A simple floor plan can be helpful if it is accurate and you have the right to publish it. It should supplement a written description rather than carry information that appears nowhere else.

Decide how availability will be maintained

If you take bookings through several channels, establish how the website will stay aligned with them. The important question is what prevents two people booking the same accommodation for overlapping dates.

You may already have a booking system that can provide the direct booking route. Before commissioning a custom calendar, ask whether the existing provider can support the journey you need.

If availability cannot be maintained reliably online, use an honest enquiry route. Explain that the request is subject to confirmation and tell the visitor what happens next. Do not display a decorative calendar that suggests live availability when nobody updates it.

Make the total booking decision understandable

Guests need to understand what they are selecting and what the price covers. Show relevant occupancy, minimum-stay arrangements and any extra charges at the point they affect the decision.

Keep booking terms consistent across the website and the system that takes the reservation. Give people a clear route to find cancellation information, arrival arrangements and answers to common practical questions.

Do not promise instant confirmation if someone must check the request manually. An automatic email acknowledging receipt is different from confirmation that the accommodation has been reserved.

Example: a family checking whether the layout works

Imagine a family planning several nights in Cheltenham with an older relative. This is an illustrative example rather than a client case study.

They want to know where the bedrooms and bathrooms are, whether there are stairs and how close a vehicle can get to the entrance. The property page provides a factual layout description, useful photographs and a contact route for anything uncertain.

They check suitable dates and follow the clearly labelled booking or enquiry process. The site helps them assess their own requirements without relying on broad labels such as "suitable for everyone".

That is a stronger customer journey than requiring them to book first and ask about the layout afterwards.

Be precise about location and arrival

For a property advertised around Cheltenham, say where it actually is. Avoid describing somewhere outside the town as central or suggesting proximity that has not been checked.

Provide useful arrival details at the appropriate stage. Public pages can explain the general location, access characteristics and transport considerations; specific entry instructions can be shared with confirmed guests through your normal process.

Local recommendations can be useful when they are current and based on genuine knowledge. Keep the core property information complete before adding a large visitor guide. A guest will usually need to understand their accommodation before choosing where to eat.

Prepare a property information checklist

Before asking a developer for a quote, gather:

  • Accurate occupancy, room layout and bed arrangements.
  • A current set of photographs with permission to publish them.
  • Factual information about steps, entrances and other access features.
  • The existing booking provider and how different channels are managed.
  • The policies and charges that must appear during booking.
  • A clear owner for availability, content changes and guest enquiries.
